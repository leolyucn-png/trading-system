ABSTRACT
Under the background of various macroeconomic policy interventions and market uncertainty, changes in investor sentiment have become the main indicator for evaluating financial markets. Drawing on large-scale social media data from China, this study systematically traces the sentiment trajectory of A-share market investors during periods of intensive macroeconomic policymaking. Using Sina Weibo as the primary data source, we collected 85,770 original user-generated comments during the key policy window from September 2024 to January 2025. Through a combined approach of trend analysis and sentiment analysis, leveraging SnowNLP and Latent Dirichlet Allocation (LDA) topic modeling, we uncover the dynamic relationship between policy signals and market sentiment. Findings reveal that policy announcements are associated with marked shifts in the structure of investor sentiment, optimism surges in the immediate aftermath of policy releases, but gradually moderates or diverges as policy effects are digested. Thematic evolution analysis further indicates that capital flows consistently constitute the market’s central concern, while topics such as policy leadership, technological innovation, and external shocks rotate in prominence across different stages. Notably, policy signals appear to coincide with amplified sentiment during certain periods, whereas sustained regulatory actions contribute to the increasing complexity and heterogeneity of sentiment structures. This study, through dynamic capture of investors' sentiment concepts in different periods, suggests that policy intervention may temporarily shape the public's emotional cognitive framework. The transparency and effectiveness of policy information can play a role in stabilizing and regulating the economic market. These patterns indicate that the transparency and timing of policy communication are closely associated with the stability of investor sentiment..


Keywords: China’s Stock Market; Macroeconomic Policies; Latent Dirichlet Allocation; Sentiment Analysis; Social media
1 Introduction
In recent years, the global macroeconomic landscape has remained volatile, characterized by escalating geopolitical risks and growing market uncertainties. In response, major economies have frequently adjusted their monetary and fiscal policies to navigate this increasingly complex environment. Against this backdrop, the operational dynamics of China’s capital market and investor behavior have undergone profound transformations. Compared with mature markets in Europe and North America, China’s A-share market is dominated by retail investors, exhibits heightened sentiment-driven volatility, and is strongly influenced by policy orientation. Policy signals often exert immediate and substantial impacts on investor sentiment and price fluctuations (Zeng et al., 2025; Lin & Qiu, 2023). Accordingly, systematically identifying and tracing the trajectory of investor sentiment under policy intervention holds substantial theoretical and practical value for understanding the functioning of China’s capital markets, enhancing policy effectiveness, and mitigating financial risks (Pástor & Veronesi, 2012; Baker et al., 2019).
On the one hand, macroeconomic policy, as a regulatory instrument, not only directly affects market liquidity through mechanisms such as interest rate adjustments, money supply, and fiscal expenditures, but also indirectly shapes investor expectations and sentiment through the release of policy signals (Kurov, 2010; Idnani et al., 2023). Recent research has highlighted policy uncertainty, the intensity of policy intervention, and expectation management as key variables influencing stock market volatility in China (Baker, Bloom, & Davis, 2016). Due to the dominance of retail investors—who are particularly emotion-driven and highly sensitive to policy information—the A-share market is often referred to as a quintessential “policy-driven market” (Zeng et al., 2025). However, it is important to note that the influence of macro policies on market sentiment is neither linear nor static. While policy announcements often induce short-term emotional surges and abrupt market reactions, investor sentiment tends to normalize—or even diverge—after the policies are digested and their outcomes become clearer (Nader Naifar et al., 2023).
On the other hand, with the rapid advancement of the Internet and digital media technologies, social media has emerged as a critical platform for investors to express opinions, convey emotions, access information, and construct financial narratives (Papacharissi, 2016; Renault, 2020). Compared to traditional news reports or survey-based instruments, social media data offers significant advantages, including real-time responsiveness, broader reach, and large-scale user participation, enabling researchers to capture investors’ emotional reactions and focal concerns during policy events with greater granularity (Bollen et al., 2010; Liu et al., 2023). Text mining, sentiment analysis, and topic modeling have been widely adopted to extract market sentiment and monitor shifts in investor behavior across platforms such as Weibo and Twitter, providing new empirical insights into the micro-foundations and macro-governance of financial markets (Kramer et al., 2014; Gong et al., 2022). Furthermore, the emotional contagion effect inherent in social media has accelerated the collective resonance and dynamic evolution of investor sentiment, positioning group-level emotions as a crucial non-rational driver of asset pricing and market volatility (Fowler & Christakis, 2008; Ferrara & Yang, 2015).
Although previous studies have revealed correlations between policy information, media sentiment, and market volatility, past analyses have predominantly focused on foreign markets or centered on news text content. Systematic empirical research combining China's domestic social media data with policy event dynamics remains scarce (Duz & Tas, 2021; Qing et al., 2023). Furthermore, existing literature rarely provides detailed depictions of the phased changes in investor sentiment and the evolution of topic structures before and after policy implementation. Addressing these gaps, this research combines trend analysis, sentiment analysis, and LDA topic modeling methods to answer the following core research questions:
(1) During the macro-policy release stage, what trajectories and structures does investor sentiment exhibit on social media platforms across different time periods?
(2) How does investors' thematic attention evolve across different policy announcement stages? What are the dominant discussion topics and focal points?
This research collects 98,717 comments related to China's stock investment market and macroeconomic policies from Sina Weibo, a major Chinese social media platform, within the timeframe from September 1, 2024 to January 28, 2025. We employed trend analysis to examine the overall dynamic changes in public comment volume. Sentiment analysis was then applied to identify investors' collective emotional states (positive, negative, or neutral) across different stages, capturing shifts in sentiment trends. Finally, Latent Dirichlet Allocation (LDA) was used to identify the discussion themes and focal points of investors during each stage. Through large-scale, high-frequency social media data mining and time-series analysis, this research aims to provide a more nuanced, multidimensional understanding of investor sentiment. It seeks to offer empirical evidence demonstrating the correlation between policy signals and market sentiment, thereby providing actionable insights for regulators on how to more effectively manage market expectations in the digital age.
2 Literature review
2.1 Macroeconomic Policies and Investor Sentiment
In recent years, persistent global turbulence has rendered the economic environment increasingly uncertain. In this context, macroeconomic policies serve as critical instruments guiding investor sentiment and shaping investment preferences (Pástor & Veronesi, 2012). Baker et al. (2019) emphasize that both policy-related and non-policy-related factors significantly affect stock market volatility, with the magnitude and nature of their influence evolving over time. Among macroeconomic policies, monetary measures (e.g., interest rate adjustments and liquidity tools) and fiscal (tax-related) interventions are widely recognized as the most influential tools for regulating stock market fluctuations (Kurov, 2010; Idnani et al., 2023; Zeng et al., 2025). Furthermore, the impact of comprehensive regulatory frameworks and specific policy categories shows marked temporal heterogeneity (Baker et al., 2019). In China's financial markets—often referred to as a "policy-driven market" (Zeng et al., 2025; Lin & Qiu, 2023)—the dominance of retail investors (exceeding 200 million individuals) amplifies the sensitivity of market sentiment to policy signals, making policy interventions particularly impactful in this context.
Although the actual regulatory effects of macroeconomic policies may be delayed, investors tend to adjust their decision-making behavior immediately in response to policy signals (Zeng et al., 2025). While investment decision-making is ostensibly a rational process based on information processing, numerous studies highlight the significant role of emotional factors in shaping investor behavior (Sul et al., 2017; Papacharissi, 2016; Idnani et al., 2023). According to Emotional Contagion Theory (ECT), emotions are inherently dynamic and contagious, evolving in reaction to external information and market events. This suggests that investor emotions can be transmitted via social media platforms, where individuals are susceptible to prevailing emotional trends. Such contagion may eventually give rise to group-level emotional resonance and opinion convergence (Fowler & Christakis, 2008; Ferrara & Yang, 2015; Kramer et al., 2014).
Investor sentiment, as a significant irrational factor influencing the objective pricing of financial stock markets, has become a key issue in stock market analysis. It reflects the collective beliefs investors hold about the value of assets, often departing from objective fundamentals, these beliefs significantly influence investment decisions, particularly when they rely on subjective judgment or are swayed by irrational drivers (Baker & Wurgler, 2007; Huang et al., 2015; Kitonyi et al., 2023; Zeng et al., 2025). The introduction of macroeconomic policies affects both investor sentiment and market expectations, thereby triggering fluctuations in economic and financial markets (Pástor & Veronesi, 2012). Supporting this perspective, Nader Naifar et al. (2023), using a Quantile-on-Quantile (QQA) approach, demonstrate that economic policy uncertainty (EPU), global financial volatility (as captured by the VIX), and media coverage during the COVID-19 pandemic significantly influenced the returns of Bitcoin and Ethereum. This finding underscores how policy-induced market fluctuations indirectly shape asset prices by altering investor sentiment.
On September 24, 2024, the People’s Bank of China (PBOC) announced a series of structural monetary policy instruments, including swap facilities for securities, funds, and insurance companies (initially totaling RMB 500 billion), as well as refinancing facilities for stock repurchases and holdings (RMB 300 billion in the first tranche). The central bank also signaled the potential for expansion depending on future developments. Two days later, on September 26, the Central Politburo announced intensified counter-cyclical adjustments through both fiscal and monetary policies. For the first time, policymakers called for the real estate market to “stabilize after a decline” and encouraged long-term institutional capital to enter the equity market. Following these intensive policy measures, investor sentiment surged: the Shanghai Composite Index jumped nearly 700 points within six trading days (September 24 to October 8), marking a cumulative increase of approximately 30% (Xinhua, 2024; People's Daily, 2024; Wind Information, 2025). This sequence of events illustrates not only the significant short-term impact of macroeconomic policies on investor sentiment and market expectations but also highlights the amplifying effect that policy signals can exert during critical periods (Fang et al., 2022).
2.2 Previous Research on Stock Market and Investor Sentiment Changes
There exists a well-documented interactive relationship between stock market dynamics and investor sentiment. Investor sentiment is widely recognized as a key contributor to the deviation of market prices from their fundamental values. Early studies primarily employed traditional sentiment indices to empirically validate the effect of investor sentiment on market volatility (Baker & Wurgler, 2007; Brown & Cliff, 2005). With the rapid advancement of the Internet and digital media technologies, social media has gradually supplanted traditional community-based communication as the dominant medium for public discourse. In this context, media sentiment has emerged as a crucial variable in investor sentiment analysis (Tetlock, 2007; Bollen et al., 2010; Liu et al., 2023).
Tetlock (2007) applied dictionary-based sentiment analysis to positive and negative opinion columns in The Wall Street Journal, revealing a dynamic relationship between media tone and stock returns. Similarly, Baker, Bloom, and Davis (2016) constructed the Economic Policy Uncertainty (EPU) Index by text-mining major U.S. newspapers and demonstrated that heightened policy uncertainty adversely affects corporate investment decisions and overall macroeconomic performance.
Although sentiment analysis based on news text is feasible, traditional media data suffer from several limitations. First, news reporting is subject to temporal delays, making it difficult to capture real-time sentiment shifts in a highly liquid financial market. Second, due to the non-original and second-hand nature of news articles, they often fail to accurately reflect investors' authentic emotional experiences. Third, the one-way communication model of traditional media limits its ability to capture sentiment interaction or contagion among investors (Sprenger et al., 2014; Fowler & Christakis, 2008; Ferrara & Yang, 2015). To overcome these limitations, researchers have shifted their focus to “first-hand” investor sentiment data—namely, user-generated content on social media platforms. For instance, Bollen et al. (2010) utilized tools such as OpinionFinder and GPOMS to analyze Twitter content and constructed public sentiment indicators to quantify investor emotions. Kramer et al. (2014), using the Linguistic Inquiry and Word Count (LIWC) method, examined the frequency of positive and negative emotion words in Facebook status updates to measure public sentiment and assess the behavioral impact of emotional contagion. Although this Facebook experiment has since drawn methodological and ethical scrutiny, its core observation of large-scale affective diffusion on social platforms has been widely echoed in subsequent work. These studies confirm the rapid and widespread diffusion of sentiment on social platforms.
Further, Gu and Kurov (2020) found that shifts in social media sentiment following Federal Reserve announcements significantly influenced short-term market volatility. Using an event study methodology, they empirically confirmed that policy disclosures exert immediate and pronounced effects on investor sentiment. Nevertheless, how media commentary might stimulate or shape actual financial trading behavior remains an open empirical question (Broadstock & Zhang, 2019). Continuous multi-perspective research into user commentary on social media platforms thus remains highly relevant and necessary.
2.3 Social Media Data Analysis of Financial Investment Sentiment
In the digital age, public perceptions of major socio-economic events are shaped not only through rational discourse and factual information, but also through emotional resonance. Papacharissi (2016) introduced the concept of affective publics, arguing that users rapidly form dynamic, networked communities through shared emotional expression and collective affect. These “publics” are not defined by geography or institutional affiliation in the traditional sense, but rather as virtual collectives formed via platforms such as Twitter.
With the accelerated penetration and deepening influence of social media, online platforms have become crucial channels for investors to voice financial opinions and convey investment sentiment—profoundly reshaping the traditional mechanisms of information dissemination and decision-making in financial markets (Renault, 2020; Broadstock & Zhang, 2019; Liu et al., 2023). Investor sentiment and opinion dynamics are recorded and disseminated in real time (e.g., through likes, comments, shares), forming observable sentiment fluctuations that offer new data sources and analytical perspectives for financial market research (Broadstock & Zhang, 2019; Renault, 2020). Rooted in behavioral finance theory, investor sentiment—often shaped by irrational psychological and emotional drivers—plays a substantial role in the mispricing of financial assets (Baker & Wurgler, 2007). Emotional fluctuations within investor communities can lead to significant deviations from fundamental values. In today’s digital media landscape, social media has become a critical medium for the transmission of such sentiment, substantially accelerating emotional contagion among investor groups (Gong et al., 2022; Duan et al., 2021). For instance, Gong et al. (2022) found that the emotional state of social media users functions as a “magnifying lens” for market volatility—small emotional shifts can rapidly propagate and influence overall sentiment at scale.
Each day, millions of users engage in discussions on social media platforms (Ferrara & Yang, 2015), and this content is instantaneously recorded and disseminated across networks, forming topic-based sentiment systems. Compared to traditional media sources and survey data, social media data offers higher frequency, larger sample size, and real-time granularity—allowing researchers to capture sentiment changes immediately following market events or policy announcements (Renault, 2020; Liu et al., 2023). This makes social media a more direct, objective, and timely tool for gauging investor sentiment (Duz & Tas, 2021).
In recent years, extensive research has been conducted to mine investor sentiment through text analysis. By employing text mining, natural language processing (NLP), sentiment analysis techniques, and deep learning methods (LDA), it has become possible to accurately extract investor sentiment indicators from social media. This approach has yielded positive results in predicting asset price movements and market volatility(Yang, 2022; Renault, 2020). 
Therefore, analyzing financial investment sentiment using social media data not only possesses sufficient scientific rigor and methodological soundness in theoretical foundations and methodology, but also effectively advances the renewal and expansion of financial market research paradigms. This approach holds significant academic innovation value and practical guidance implications. However, the majority of investor sentiment research samples currently originate from foreign social media platforms such as Twitter and StockTwits (Bollen et al., 2010; Duz & Tas, 2021; Sul et al., 2017; Sprenger et al., 2014), while studies on China's mainstream domestic social platforms like Weibo, Douyin, and Xiaohongshu remain scarce (Qing et al., 2023). In summary, this research addresses gaps in existing research by analyzing investor sentiment trajectories under policy interventions through LDA thematic modeling of raw user comments on Chinese social platforms, thereby filling regional, data, and methodological gaps.

3.1 Data Collection
3.1.1 Original comment source
This research selected Sina Weibo as its data source based on the following considerations: (1) It boasts a massive user base with a distinctly youthful demographic. As of 2024, Sina Weibo's monthly active users exceeded 598 million, with users aged 18-35 accounting for over 70% (Sina Weibo, 2024; Miao et al., 2021), aligning closely with the recent trend of younger investors in China's stock market; (2) As one of China's largest comprehensive social media platforms, Sina Weibo not only covers a broader user base (Liu et al., 2022; Ye et al., 2020), but also features users frequently engaging in real-time discussions about major domestic and international economic and social events. Its real-time and highly interactive nature enables more timely and authentic capture of investor sentiment trajectories and the interactive transmission effects among users (Wu et al., 2021; Tang & Fang, 2025). (3) Compared to specialized financial communities or vertical platforms like Snowball and East Money Stock Forum, Sina Weibo features a more diversified user structure. It encompasses not only professional institutional investors and financial practitioners but also a large number of non-professional individual investors (retail investors) (Miao et al., 2021), making it more suitable for examining the sentiment characteristics and transmission mechanisms of different investor groups in China's stock market under macroeconomic policy shocks (Tang & Fang, 2025).
3.1.2 Text Data Acquisition
The data collection period spanned from September 1, 2024, to January 28, 2025. This time frame comprehensively covered key junctures before, during, and after the release of a series of major macroeconomic policies, enabling the capture of investors' immediate reactions and dynamic shifts in sentiment. Utilizing a Python-based web crawler developed in-house, text data was scraped using keywords such as “central bank,” “monetary policy,” “fiscal policy,” “securities market,” “Chinese stock market,” and “A-shares.” A total of 98,717 raw comment data points were obtained. Collected information included textual content (specific comment text), comment volume (number of replies to a topic), like counts (likes received by comments), user IDs, and comment posting times. Notably, stock-related topics involve numerous distinct financial concepts and terminology. Therefore, we sought assistance from researchers specializing in economics to establish a selection of proper nouns. During comment collection, we focused on whether the content included three key elements: policy-related content, financial/stock-related keywords, and statements conveying personal subjective sentiment.
3.1.3 Text Data Preprocessing
The raw data underwent a rigorous preprocessing workflow, with specific operations including: removing duplicate comments (deleting duplicate comment content based on user ID), filtering out advertisements and irrelevant information (such as images, links, @ symbols, and emojis), and applying Chinese word segmentation using the Jieba tool in Python. A Chinese stopword list was constructed and applied to filter out terms unrelated to the research topic. After cleaning and processing, a total of 85,770 valid comment records were obtained.
3.2 Data Analysis
3.2.1 Discussing Trends in Investor Sentiment Across Different Time Periods
Trend Analysis
Given the distinct time-sensitive and phased nature of policy announcements, the primary step in identifying shifts in investor sentiment perception is to clarify the trajectory of discussion volume (number of relevant posts and comments published) triggered before and after specific policy events. This research statistically analyzes the volume of policy-related posts and comments on social media platforms before and after policy announcements (Sukhavasi et al., 2023; Liu et al., 2025). Utilizing Trend Analysis methodology, it visually presents the dynamic evolution of investor discussion intensity across different time stages through graphical representations. Specifically, during the initial stage of policy release, investor discussions surge rapidly, with comment content concentrated around a few core keywords, reflecting the significant stimulating effect of the policy event on market sentiment and attention. Subsequently, discussion volume gradually stabilizes, indicating sustained high investor attention toward the policy topic.
Sentiment Analysis
Sentiment analysis aims to identify textual materials on social platforms and quantify investors' emotional tendencies toward the stock market before and after macroeconomic policy announcements. Sentiment analysis has been extensively studied and applied in conjunction with social media comment texts (Sukhavasi et al. 2023). For instance, Qi et al. (2024) employed models such as SnowNLP and Bayesian LDA to conduct sentiment and topic analysis on social media data, revealing that both the Chinese public and healthcare professionals generally hold negative attitudes toward nurse prescribing authority. This research employs SnowNLP, a Python-based Chinese sentiment analysis tool, for sentiment classification. SnowNLP automates sentiment analysis of Chinese text, leveraging its robust semantic recognition and sentiment classification capabilities for Chinese. Consequently, it is widely adopted in sentiment analysis research involving Chinese social media data (Wang et al., 2025).
3.2.2 Identifying the Evolution of Key Themes Across Different Stages of Investor Development
Latent Dirichlet Allocation, LDA
LDA is an unsupervised topic modeling algorithm widely used for extracting latent themes from large-scale textual datasets (Wang et al., 2025). In this study, the optimal number of topics was determined based on the topic coherence score, which evaluates the semantic consistency of words within each topic. The use of coherence scores ensures that the resulting topic structure is both interpretable and representative of the underlying discourse. This allows the model to capture meaningful thematic patterns in investor sentiment and relate them to specific macroeconomic policy contexts (Xin & Ren, 2023). Across the candidate range of topic numbers, the coherence score was highest at four topics, so a four-topic solution (k = 4) was adopted for each stage, consistent with the four themes reported for every stage in Figures 6–9. The corresponding Cᵥ coherence at k = 4 was 0.45, 0.22, 0.27, and 0.38 for Stages 1 through 4, respectively (range 0.22–0.45).
4 Result
4.1 The dynamic trajectory of comment volume and policy evolution
 Stock market discussion volume saw two notable surges in October and November 2024, particularly during the National Day holiday period. Specific events included: On September 24, 2024, the People's Bank of China announced a reserve requirement ratio cut and interest rate reduction, while introducing swap facilities for securities, fund, and insurance companies, along with a special re-lending tool for stock buybacks and share repurchases to release long-term liquidity and stabilize capital markets; Subsequently, on September 26, the Political Bureau of the CPC Central Committee meeting emphasized efforts to boost capital markets and vigorously guide medium-to-long-term capital into the market. In November, the China Securities Regulatory Commission (CSRC) issued new rules on market capitalization management for listed companies. The Central Economic Work Conference in December explicitly set the goal of “stabilizing the property and stock markets,” reinforcing proactive fiscal and accommodative monetary policies to promote asset price recovery and improve the asset-liability conditions of households and enterprises. Stage 1: Pre-Policy Announcement Period (September 1, 2024–September 23, 2024)
As shown in Figure 3, time-trend analysis reveals that discussions about China's stock market on social media exhibited minor fluctuations from early to mid-September 2024. By mid-September, the volume of related comments surged sharply, reaching a peak.

On September 24, 2024, the People's Bank of China implemented reserve requirement ratio and interest rate cuts. Subsequently, the Politburo meeting on September 26 explicitly emphasized supportive policies for capital markets. During this stage, central macroeconomic policies were released intensively, sending strong and concentrated policy signals. Consequently, investor discussions on social media surged significantly, peaking from late September to early October (as shown in Figure 3). Stage 3: Policy Effect Observation Stage (October 28, 2024 – December 16, 2024)
From the implementation of the mortgage interest rate adjustment policy on October 31, 2024, to the introduction of new market value management regulations on November 15, the frequency of policy announcements decreased during this stage. However, the content of policies gradually shifted toward specific refinement and implementation. Investor commentary volume decreased overall compared to the previous stage but remained higher than pre-policy levels, reflecting sustained observation and rational assessment of policy outcomes. Market sentiment gradually shifted from optimistic expectations to cautious observation, with investor sentiment becoming more rational.
Stage 4: Policy Stabilization and Consolidation Stage (December 17, 2024 – January 28, 2025)
The Central Economic Work Conference on December 12, 2024 emphasized market stability. Subsequently, on January 23, 2025, multiple departments jointly proposed an initiative to attract long-term capital inflows, marking the entry into a stage of deepening and stabilizing policy adjustments. During this period, the volume of investor discussions gradually returned to a stable range. Market focus shifted from short-term policy stimulus to the long-term effectiveness of policies and the stability of the macroeconomic environment. This reflects a calming of investor sentiment, with the market as a whole entering a stage of rational deepening regarding policy outcomes.

4.2 The dynamic trajectory of investor sentiment
In the section analyzing the dynamic changes in investor sentiment, we utilized the SnowNLP sentiment analysis tool to examine the comments of Internet users, extracting positive, negative, and neutral sentiment changes regarding the Chinese stock market. As shown in Figure 4, the analysis results reveal significant fluctuations in investor sentiment in the Chinese stock market over four different periods, a process that aligns with the timing of the introduction of important policies (see Figure 1). Cross-analysis of multi-dimensional data reveals that policy signals and market expectations have been dynamically associated with investors' market sentiment (positive or negative). During the period from September 1 to September 23, 2024, negative sentiment dominated (57.3%), while positive sentiment (18.8%) remained low. During this period, no favorable policies had been announced, leading to insufficient market confidence. Negative sentiment dominated while positive sentiment remained low, indicating that investors generally adopted a wait-and-see and cautious attitude toward stock investments, with the overall market shrouded in uncertainty.
The second stage (September 24 to October 27, 2024) coincided with a peak in policy announcements, triggering a dramatic shift in investor sentiment structure. The proportion of positive comments surged significantly to 53.7%. This abrupt change closely coincided with the intensive rollout of policies, such as the People's Bank of China's reserve requirement ratio and interest rate cuts, and strong policy signals from the Central Political Bureau meeting, rapidly unleashing investor optimism.
Stage Three (October 28–December 16, 2024) saw a rebalancing of sentiment. The proportion of positive sentiment began to decline (down 17.0%). As policies were implemented, the focus shifted to execution (e.g., mortgage rate adjustments, new market capitalization management rules), and initial optimism gradually gave way to rational assessment. Investors began to engage in more rational reflection and reassessment of the actual effectiveness of policies and the macroeconomic environment. 
Stage Four (December 17, 2024 – January 28, 2025) saw further intensification of sentiment divergence. Negative sentiment continued to rise to 47.5%. This stage followed the Central Economic Work Conference's emphasis on “stabilizing the property and stock markets” and multi-departmental efforts to promote long-term capital inflows. However, the resurgence of negative sentiment indicates that investors remain highly skeptical about the sustainability of policy effects, the resilience of market recovery, and external uncertainties. This suggests a “return to rationality” or a resurgence of “skepticism.”

Notably, terms like ‘China’ and “consumer” indicate that investment analysis is increasingly integrating China's domestic market and consumption-driven factors.
Theme Two revolves around market reversals and cyclical shifts. Investors exhibit heightened sensitivity to inflection points in market volatility. Terms like “poor,” “fall,” and “end” reflect retrospective views on prior market downturns, while ‘rebound’ and “bull market” convey anticipation for future recovery and upward momentum. Cyclical fluctuations in investor sentiment strongly correlate with varying degrees of optimism or pessimism regarding impending market turning points. Theme Three (Industry Performance) consistently reflects investors' structural focus on sectors like technology, semiconductors, real estate, finance, and new energy. Theme Four highlights the impact of external macroeconomic and environmental variables on financial markets. Terms like “American” and ‘China’ indicate investors consider international relations and geopolitical factors as key economic predictors, while words such as “Spring Festival” reveal their attention to seasonal events and their short-term market effects.

5 Discussion
This research aims to analyze comment texts on social media platforms, systematically exploring two core questions: First, what are the dynamic trajectories and structural patterns of investor sentiment under macro-policy interventions (RQ1)? Second, how does the underlying collective focus of investor attention evolve (RQ2)? This chapter will provide an in-depth interpretation of the research findings around these two questions, elucidating their theoretical contributions and practical implications.
5.1 Analysis of Investor Sentiment Trajectories
5.1.1 Policy Signals and Investor Sentiment Fluctuations
Research findings indicate that following each policy announcement, investor sentiment experiences short-term, rapid fluctuations (Guo & Xie, 2024; Yao & Li, 2020). In the second stage, the volume of online comments surges significantly, reflecting heightened investor attention to the policy event. Both the proportion of positive sentiment and the average sentiment score exhibit a sharp increase, forming a stark contrast to the negative sentiment observed in the first stage and reaching the peak across the entire study period. This directly addresses the “trajectory” inquiry in RQ1. Drawing on Emotional Contagion Theory, emotions on social media platforms are not isolated personal experiences but exhibit dynamic, interactive contagiousness (Maurya et al., 2025). The policy announcement rapidly spread and amplified through social platforms, where differing user perspectives interacted to form a potent emotional information network. Users not only disseminated information but were simultaneously influenced by the prevailing network sentiment, ultimately generating large-scale affective resonance within a short timeframe (Yao & Li, 2020).
5.1.2 From emotional fluctuations to a return to rationality
Investors who gather on online platforms due to shared positive emotions (hope, optimism) form temporary online investor groups that can be described as the “Affective Publics” proposed by Papacharissi (2016). The Affective Publics are not grounded in rational identity or shared interests but are purely sustained by shared emotional experiences. As explained earlier, Chinese stock investors are predominantly retail investors who are not professional financial experts. Therefore, at different stages, investors are not rationally valuing policy terms but are participating as members of the internet “Affective Publics” in a large-scale emotional expression and carnival (Pan et al., 2022). Because emotional expressions are not decisions made through professional assessment, positive sentiment began to decline significantly in the third stage, with negative and neutral sentiments returning. The market sentiment structure shifted from highly homogeneous optimism back to a more differentiated and cautious negative state (Yao & Li, 2020; Li, 2015). This “rise-fall” oscillatory trajectory extends Baker & Wurgler's (2007) investor sentiment theory. While prior research primarily focused on constructing cross-sectional sentiment indicators and validating their impact on asset prices, our study adds a crucial time-series dynamic dimension to this theory. It reveals that sentiment itself, as a variable, undergoes an intrinsic lifecycle process triggered by external shocks.
5.2 Shift in Investor Focus
5.2.1 From “Macro Policies” to “Micro Assessments”
The LDA results demonstrated that investor attention underwent a process of shifting from macro to micro levels. In the second stage, policy signals appeared to play a powerful guiding role, with the collective attention of the stock market converging from dispersed topics—such as the Federal Reserve, leading technology companies, domestic technology industries, and Sino-US trade relations—toward the central theme of “national policy guidelines.” This convergence formed a consensus within the investors' cognitive framework. As illustrated in Figure 7, market cognition became highly homogeneous, with all information releases centered around policy as the core topic for discussion and interpretation. This high cognitive alignment also fostered collective sentiment. However, as the market entered the third and fourth stages, we observed a trend of “rational reversion” (Guo et al., 2023). Investors began moving beyond the surface content of policies to deeply analyze underlying risks, shifting their focus toward more pragmatic foundational topics like “external factors” and “sector performance.” This reflects a shift among Chinese stock investors from receiving macro signals to exploring micro intrinsic value—a cognitive transition from “policy narratives” to “financial markets.” Market participants should not be simplistically viewed as “irrational followers.” Investors' thematic focus and emotional behaviors can serve as guiding beacons for financial market forecasting. (Pástor & Veronesi, 2012) mathematically examined how policy uncertainty impacts asset prices, yet the underlying role of investor cognition remains under-explored. Our research illuminates this cognitive process: investors do not passively absorb uncertainty but actively engage in collective meaning-seeking activities.
5.2.2 “Funding Trends” Remain a Constant Focus for Investors 
Although investor themes exhibit distinct phased patterns according to LDA model analysis, as shown in Figure 10, “Funding Trend” persists across all time periods as a continuous focus for investors. Compared to other financial markets, China's A-share market features a massive proportion of retail investors. For this large group, conducting in-depth research into target companies' financial statements and business models (i.e., fundamental analysis) incurs high time costs with uncertain outcomes. In contrast, “funding flow” data serves as an exceptionally concise, intuitive, and accessible decision signal. Investors can intuitively construct their investment selections based on which stocks are being heavily purchased (Guo et al., 2023). Throughout China's stock market evolution over the past three decades, major bull market cycles have frequently commenced and peaked in tandem with macro-level monetary easing policies and massive capital inflows, rather than being driven purely by corporate profit growth. Consequently, this potent historical experience has left an indelible mark on investors' minds through the “path dependency” effect (Guo et al., 2023). In China, liquidity remains the most fundamental and potent force driving market trends. This collective memory causes investors to unconsciously prioritize the question “Is money flowing in?” over the fundamental analysis of “Is the company performing well?” when evaluating the market.
6 Limitations and future work
Although this study provides a new direction for analyzing and interpreting the Chinese stock market and investors, we still need to clarify its limitations. Firstly, the singularity of the data source. The data of this study is entirely derived from the comment texts on Sina Weibo. Although Weibo is one of the mainstream social platforms in China with a large user base, its user profile may be more inclined towards young, active and less experienced individual investors, and neglects the emotions and viewpoints of more professional financial investors. This may lead to the research results overestimating the market's emotional volatility to a certain extent. Further research could consider cross-platform comparative analysis, including more professional financial communities such as Xueqiu and Eastmoney Stock Forum within the research scope, to test whether the sentiment and theme evolution patterns discovered in this study are equally universal on specialized platforms, and to explore the differences among different communities.
Secondly, there is a certain limitation in the inference of causal relationships. This study demonstrates and explains through time series analysis, revealing a strong correlation between policy signals and emotional evolution. However, from a methodological perspective, it still falls under the category of correlation research. Moreover, because the present analysis relies on descriptive trend and proportion comparisons rather than formal statistical inference (e.g., hypothesis testing, confidence intervals, or effect-size estimation), the reported relationships between policy signals and sentiment shifts should be interpreted as descriptive and correlational rather than causal. To establish a stronger causal relationship, future research can adopt more sophisticated quasi-natural experimental designs.
Finally, the analytical methods still have limitations. Although natural language processing technology is already very powerful, it is still difficult to fully capture complex language phenomena such as irony and puns, which may lead to minor deviations in emotion classification. More importantly, this study regards investors as a homogeneous group, but in reality, there are significant differences in the cognitive and behavioral patterns of investors. Future research can move towards more in-depth heterogeneity analysis. For instance, it can attempt to use users' historical posts, follow lists and other information to distinguish different groups such as value investors, trend investors and short-term traders, and compare the differences in their response patterns under policy shocks. In addition, multimodal analysis (combining information from text, images, and videos) and cross-national comparison (comparing the response patterns of different markets such as China and the United States to similar policies) are also highly promising exploration directions. Two measurement caveats also warrant emphasis. First, SnowNLP’s sentiment model is trained largely on general-purpose and e-commerce Chinese text rather than financial discourse, so domain mismatch may misclassify finance-specific expressions; because no hand-labeled financial validation set was constructed, the reported sentiment proportions should be read as indicative rather than exact, and future work should validate the classifier against a manually annotated financial subset or adopt domain-adapted models such as FinBERT. Second, the topic-coherence (cᵥ) values at k = 4 ranged from 0.22 to 0.45 across the four stages; the comparatively low value in Stage 2 (0.22) indicates that its four topics are less internally consistent, so those topic labels should be interpreted with caution as researcher-assisted readings rather than fully data-driven clusters.

7 Conclusion
This study traced the dynamic trajectory of A-share investor sentiment on Sina Weibo across a five-month window of intensive macroeconomic policymaking (September 2024–January 2025). Combining trend analysis, SnowNLP sentiment classification, and LDA topic modeling over 85,770 comments, it documented a four-stage pattern in which optimism rose sharply around the late-September policy cluster and then moderated and diverged as the measures were absorbed, while attention to capital flows persisted as a constant focus and themes such as policy leadership and external factors rotated in prominence across stages. The contribution is primarily descriptive: it adds a time-series, stage-based lens to investor-sentiment research and illustrates how affective publics form and dissipate around policy events on Chinese social media. These findings are correlational rather than causal, rest on a single platform skewed toward younger retail users, and depend on sentiment- and topic-modeling tools whose limitations are noted above; they are therefore best read as an empirical mapping that motivates more rigorous, inference-oriented, and cross-platform research. More broadly, the study illustrates how high-frequency social-media traces can complement market-based sentiment measures for tracking how retail investors collectively interpret policy in emerging markets, and it offers a replicable, stage-based template that subsequent work can extend—through formal statistical inference, multi-platform data, and investor-heterogeneity analysis—to move from description toward explanation.
